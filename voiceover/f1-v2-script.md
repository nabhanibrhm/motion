# F1 explainer v2: draft script

Characters: MOG (guide), WEC (F1 drama fan), BOG (curious newcomer). Lines never overlap. Pause markers are seconds of silence added after the preceding line.

## hook: The Hook

*(visual: busy social feed collage of F1 posts, then it settles)*

BOG: Okay, quick question. [pause 0.3] Since when did everyone become an F1 fan?

[pause 0.4]

WEC: Since always! Look, I've got tickets!

*(action: WEC bursts in from the side, waving two race tickets; BOG leans back)*

[pause 0.4]

MOG: Well, not always. Today, Formula 1 feels like it's at the center of the universe. It's everywhere. Your friends watch it, it dominates your social media feeds, and the drivers are global cultural icons.

[pause 0.4]

BOG: Funny. I never got the memo.

MOG: Fair enough. Because rewind just fifteen years, and Formula 1 was a completely different world.

[pause 0.4]

MOG: The ultimate "old boys' club." A highly exclusive, Euro-centric billionaire's playground. You either grew up watching it... or you simply didn't care.

*(visual: sepia-toned paddock photo card, framed, with the words "Old boys' club")*

[pause 0.8]

MOG: So how did a sport wrapped in elitism and complex engineering suddenly become the most talked-about entertainment brand on the planet?

[pause 0.4]

WEC: Ooh. Good question.

MOG: Let's go and find out.

[pause 1.0]

## liberty: The Turning Point

*(action: a tall velvet-rope door slides in; BOG stops in front of it, WEC tiptoes up and peeks through the keyhole)*

MOG: To understand the boom, we have to talk about 2017. [pause 0.3] For decades, Formula 1 was run by Bernie Ecclestone.

*(visual: framed portrait card, "Bernie Ecclestone")*

MOG: Bernie was brilliant at making F1 rich. But his philosophy was all about exclusivity. The sport relied on expensive TV packages, luxury watch sponsors, and artificial scarcity.

[pause 0.4]

BOG: So the door was just... closed?

MOG: Pretty much. Social media? Drivers were essentially banned from sharing behind-the-scenes footage in the paddock. The sport was aggressively keeping the modern world out.

WEC: I can't see anything. Not even a little bit.

*(action: WEC presses an eye to the door and gives up, shoulders dropping)*

[pause 1.0]

MOG: Then, Liberty Media bought Formula 1 for 8 billion dollars.

*(visual: stat card "$8 billion")*

[hold 2.5]

MOG: They immediately realized something crucial. [pause 0.3] F1 had a massive branding problem. But it also possessed the greatest untold stories in sports.

[pause 0.4]

MOG: So Liberty stopped treating Formula 1 like a traditional racing series, and started treating it like a global media company. They completely tore down the digital walls.

*(action: MOG pushes the same door; it swings wide open and light spills out)*

[pause 0.8]

WEC: Wait, is that a phone? Drivers are allowed phones now?

*(action: WEC pulls out a phone and holds it up to film)*

MOG: Encouraged, actually. On YouTube, Instagram, and TikTok.

*(visual: YouTube, Instagram, TikTok logos appear beside the open door)*

BOG: Whoa. I can see the garage from here.

[pause 0.4]

MOG: F1 went from a closed-door society to an always-on reality show.

[pause 1.0]

## netflix: The Netflix Effect

*(action: a framed story board of driver portraits slides in; WEC carries it, proud)*

MOG: But the real rocket fuel was a little show you might have heard of. [pause 0.3] Drive to Survive.

*(visual: Drive to Survive title card)*

[pause 0.4]

MOG: Before 2019, if you were a new fan tuning into a race, you just saw twenty identical-looking cars driving in circles. You had to understand aerodynamics and tire degradation to enjoy it.

*(action: BOG squints at a row of twenty identical cars on a chart)*

BOG: Yeah. That was me. I counted twenty cars and zero reasons to care.

[pause 0.4]

MOG: Then Netflix changed the formula. They focused entirely on the humans inside the machines.

WEC: Now you've got ego, pressure, rivalries! It's basically a soap opera at three hundred kilometers an hour.

*(action: WEC taps a driver photo on the story board; BOG leans in)*

BOG: Okay, but wait. Who am I supposed to root for?

[pause 0.4]

MOG: That's exactly the point. Suddenly, you weren't watching cars. You were watching ambition, ego, massive pressure, and bitter rivalries. You learned what was at stake for the guy fighting for tenth place just to keep his job.

[pause 0.4]

BOG: Huh. Okay. Now I want to know how tenth place turns out.

[pause 0.8]

MOG: Drive to Survive gave audiences a reason to care. People who had never watched a motorsport in their lives were suddenly canceling their Sunday plans to watch Max Verstappen and Lewis Hamilton battle it out.

*(visual: framed photo card of Verstappen and Hamilton side by side)*

[pause 0.4]

MOG: F1 realized that people don't connect with lap times. [pause 0.4] They connect with stories.

[pause 1.0]

## demo: The Demographic Revolution

*(action: MOG steps up beside a large bar chart; BOG and WEC sit on either side)*

MOG: That storytelling strategy completely rewired who watches Formula 1. In just a few years, the sport experienced one of the fastest demographic shifts in modern history.

[pause 0.4]

BOG: Let me guess. Engineering nerds, mostly.

MOG: Let's check.

[pause 0.4]

MOG: The average fan age plummeted from 36 to 32.

*(visual: chart, average fan age bar drops from 36 to 32)*

[hold 2.5]

MOG: And today, a massive 43 percent of all F1 fans are under 35.

*(visual: chart, "43% under 35" highlighted)*

[hold 2.5]

WEC: And they're not all here for the same reason. Some come for the drivers. Some for the strategy. Some just for the atmosphere. Everyone walks in through a different door.

*(action: WEC points at the chart, then at BOG)*

BOG: Huh. So it's not one kind of fan.

[pause 0.4]

MOG: Not even close. The biggest shift? The female fanbase. Women now make up 42 percent of F1's global audience, driving a cultural evolution.

*(visual: chart, "42% women" segment highlighted)*

[hold 2.5]

MOG: F1 is no longer just about engine suppliers. It's embedded in fashion, lifestyle, and pop culture.

[pause 0.4]

MOG: We're seeing major beauty brands like Charlotte Tilbury and Elemis sponsoring the F1 Academy. The season launches have turned into massive stadium concerts.

*(visual: framed collage, F1 Academy sponsor logos and a season launch photo, with negative space)*

[pause 0.4]

WEC: So it's not just about the cars. I did not see that coming.

[pause 0.4]

MOG: Formula 1 isn't just a race weekend anymore. [pause 0.4] It's a lifestyle that Gen Z wants to be a part of.

[pause 1.0]

## usa: The American Dream

*(action: a large map unfolds on a table; WEC and BOG lean over it, MOG holds one corner flat)*

MOG: And we can't ignore the United States. [pause 0.3] For decades, F1 tried and failed to crack the American market.

[pause 0.4]

BOG: So what changed?

MOG: Fast forward to today, and the US hosts three of the most glamorous races on the calendar. Austin, Miami, and Vegas.

*(action: WEC slaps a ticket on Austin; BOG pins Miami; MOG marks Vegas. Three pins on the map)*

*(visual: map pins labeled Austin, Miami, Las Vegas)*

[pause 0.4]

WEC: Road trip! I'll drive. Well, someone's driving.

BOG: I'll bring snacks.

[pause 0.8]

MOG: Big tech has noticed, too. Apple recently secured a massive 150-million-dollar-a-year deal to broadcast F1 in the US.

*(visual: stat card "$150 million a year, Apple")*

[hold 2.5]

MOG: And Brad Pitt's mega-budget F1 movie dominated the global box office.

*(visual: framed movie poster card)*

[pause 0.4]

MOG: The sport has saturated every layer of media.

[pause 1.0]

## outro: Conclusion

*(action: the original velvet-rope door returns, standing wide open; WEC clutches the tickets, BOG stands in the doorway)*

MOG: Fifteen years ago, Formula 1 survived by locking its doors and asking you to pay for a glimpse inside.

[pause 0.4]

MOG: Today, it's booming because it threw the doors wide open.

[pause 0.8]

BOG: So... when's the next race?

*(action: BOG turns to the others; WEC grins and fans out two tickets)*

WEC: Already got the tickets.

[pause 0.4]

BOG: Wait, you only have two.

MOG: I'll watch from the front row. Apparently everyone gets one now.

[pause 0.8]

MOG: By focusing on the people beneath the helmets and embracing the digital age, F1 stopped being just a race. It became the greatest traveling circus on Earth.

[pause 0.6]

MOG: And now, everyone has a front-row seat.

*(action: all three look out toward the viewer; door and tickets hold in frame)*

[hold 2.0]
